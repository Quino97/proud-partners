About Page:


Copy:


It began with a certificate.
The story of Proud Partners

Sometimes, the most meaningful ideas have the simplest beginnings. For Proud Partners, it was a certificate presented by a woman named Selina Radebe to her long-time employer, Isabel Verwey.

On it were the words: "In recognition of 20 years of partnership."

Neither could have imagined how far those words would travel.

A partnership worth celebrating

In 1994, Isabel left Johannesburg to marry a Capetonian. It meant leaving behind her career at the SABC, her friends, her family and a city she loved. But one of the people she missed most was Selina, who had worked in her household for 13 years.

Fortunately, Selina felt the same way. Before long, she and her four children had also moved to Cape Town.

Some years later, Selina surprised Isabel with a certificate celebrating the twentieth anniversary of the day she had first knocked on the door of Isabel's Hillbrow flat, looking for work.

Isabel was initially intrigued by the word partnership. But on reflection, she realised how appropriate it was. Their relationship extended beyond employment to include concern for each other's families, shared experiences and the everyday challenges of life.

It also gave Isabel an idea. What if more South Africans recognised the value of working together, looking beyond their differences to find common ground?

From an idea to a purpose

In 2004, a visit to Khayelitsha in the Western Cape brought that idea into sharper focus.

The founding members of Proud Partners were deeply affected by the living conditions they encountered. Yet they were equally struck by the optimism, goodwill and determination of the people they met.

They realised that making a meaningful difference required more than good intentions. It required people to become personally involved.

And so, Proud Partners was born.

The first steps

With the initial financial support of Plexus Asset Management, Proud Partners was able to establish itself and begin putting its ideas into practice.

In 2005, employees of Plexus and their families took their commitment a step further, participating in a two-day charity walk in Ceres. Together, they set themselves a target of 1,000 kilometres, raising an impressive R80,000 through sponsorships.

It was an early demonstration of the Proud Partners philosophy: people coming together, giving their time and energy and making something happen.

A shared commitment.

From the beginning, Proud Partners was founded on a simple principle: real change happens when people become involved in one another's lives.

Rather than merely collecting and distributing donations, the organisation encourages practical cooperation between South Africans from different backgrounds and communities.

Whether through schools, community initiatives or individual relationships, the emphasis has always been on sharing knowledge, offering guidance, contributing skills and building understanding.

Because a successful partnership should enrich the lives of everyone involved.

The story continues

More than two decades later, Proud Partners has played a role in projects spanning education, music, childcare and community development.

Some of those projects have come to an end, leaving behind achievements and memories worth celebrating. Others continue to make a difference today.

Its three principal ongoing initiatives are Kinders vir die Koning, PJO Art Tracks and the Consular Ball.

Each has its own story. All share the belief that inspired Proud Partners in the first place.

Because partnership is not simply something we believe in. It's something we do.

Kinders vir die Koning Page:

Copy:




Kinders vir die Koning


From a community hall to a school of their own.

In 1995, a small group of children began gathering in a community hall in Casablanca, near Gordon's Bay. There were no proper classrooms, precious few resources and an enormous need for early childhood care and education.

But there were people who believed that every child deserved a good start in life.

More than thirty years later, that modest beginning has grown into Kinders vir die Koning, an established early childhood development centre with a brand-new school building and a remarkable story of determination, generosity and partnership.

Proud Partners is privileged to have played a part in that journey.

Where the need was greatest

For many families in Casablanca and neighbouring Sercor Park, everyday life presents considerable challenges. Unemployment, poverty and social difficulties often place enormous pressure on parents and children alike.

Some young children have had limited opportunities for early learning and development, while older siblings have sometimes had to miss school to care for them.

Without the right opportunities, children can enter formal schooling without the skills and confidence they need, starting on the back foot and potentially falling behind from the start. 

It was a need that could not be ignored.

Two mornings a week. A world of possibilities.

In 1995, Lynette van Dellen, a retired lecturer and member of the Dutch Reformed Church in Gordon's Bay, began a small children's programme in Casablanca.

With the support of a dedicated group of women, she established a preschool playgroup in the local community hall. Twice a week, children gathered for developmental activities, play and a nutritious snack.

There were few resources, but no shortage of commitment. Lynette devoted countless unpaid hours to helping the children prepare for school and giving them opportunities they might otherwise have missed.

As the initiative grew, so did the need for proper facilities and more structured teaching.

In 2007, Kinders vir die Koning became a registered non-profit organisation, with Elsa Verwey and social worker Wilma Piek among those who helped establish it.

Making room for everyone

In 2012, a donated container became the unlikely home of a Grade R classroom.

Cornelia Truter, a retired pre-primary school principal, joined Lynette in preparing the children for formal schooling. Their focus extended beyond the alphabet and numbers to the social, emotional, physical and perceptual skills children need when entering a classroom.

But there was another problem.

While the six-year-olds were receiving lessons in the container, the younger children who accompanied them had nowhere to go.

Proud Partners recognised an opportunity to help.

The organisation established a preschool playgroup called Proud Pellies, appointed three women from the community as educators and undertook to pay their monthly salaries.

Before long, more than 80 children between the ages of three and six were attending developmental activities in the community hall four mornings a week.

For the children, it meant a safer, more stimulating start. For the three women, it also meant employment and an opportunity to contribute directly to their community.

From containers to classrooms

The little Grade R classroom continued to flourish, even when conditions were far from comfortable.

In winter, the container could be bitterly cold, with rain turning the surrounding ground to mud. In summer, temperatures inside became so unbearable that lessons sometimes had to take place outdoors.

Something had to change.

In 2014, Proud Partners contributed R17,000 towards improving the facilities. Together with support from other donors, sufficient funds were raised to provide two new Nutec classrooms.

Two years later, in 2016, a further four classrooms were acquired with the assistance of sponsors.

What had once seemed an impossible dream was gradually taking shape.

A little diplomacy. A lot of goodwill.

Partnership can take many forms, sometimes from unexpected quarters.

In 2019, Proud Partners introduced the school to the Russian Consulate General, facilitating a meeting with Consul General Roman Ambarov and his team.

The Consulate subsequently donated a laptop and video projector to support the school's educational activities.

At the handover ceremony, the children entertained their visitors with songs before enjoying a screening of the Russian animated series Masha and the Bear.

It was a memorable occasion and another example of how a simple introduction can lead to something worthwhile.

At last, a place to call their own

For decades, the dream had been to give Kinders vir die Koning a permanent home.

A site had been donated in 2009, but bringing the building project to completion proved a long and difficult undertaking. Funding constraints, construction challenges and unexpected setbacks repeatedly delayed progress.

Then, in 2026, the dream finally became reality.

A contribution of R6.15 million from the Tirisano Construction Trust, facilitated through the ASLA Foundation, provided the crucial funding for the new school building.

On 7 September 2026, the children moved into their new home in Sercor Park.

Exactly one month later, on 7 October, the school celebrated its official opening, bringing together the people and organisations who had contributed to its remarkable journey.

Isabel Verwey attended on behalf of the Verwey family and Proud Partners, lighting a candle in remembrance of those whose dedication had helped make the day possible.

From a community hall and a converted container to a permanent school building, it was a milestone worth celebrating.

And a new beginning.

A partnership that continues

Over the years, Proud Partners' involvement with Kinders vir die Koning has extended from establishing Proud Pellies and supporting additional classrooms to helping secure educational equipment and contributing towards educators' salaries.

The organisation has also supported the school's website and its ongoing maintenance.

It is a relationship that has endured because of a shared belief in the importance of early childhood development and the power of people working together.

Today, Kinders vir die Koning continues to serve the children and families of Casablanca and Sercor Park.

The buildings may have changed beyond recognition, but the idea that started it all remains wonderfully simple.

Give children a good beginning, and you give them a better chance at everything that follows.

#EndPeriodPoverty 

A period shouldn't interrupt an education.

Imagine having to choose between buying food and buying sanitary products. Or missing lectures because you simply cannot afford the essentials.

For many South African university students, period poverty is a very real barrier to education.

In 2023, Stellenbosch University introduced its #EndPeriodPoverty initiative to help address this challenge, installing sanitary pad dispensing machines on its campuses to give students free, convenient and discreet access to essential menstrual products.

The initiative forms part of the University's Bridge the Gap programme, which helps students overcome financial challenges that could otherwise stand in the way of their studies.

With three dispensing machines designed to supply approximately 1,100 packs of sanitary pads every month, the programme is helping remove one unnecessary worry from students' lives.

Recognising the importance of this initiative, Proud Partners began contributing R10,000 annually in 2024, an ongoing commitment to helping students focus on their education rather than the affordability of basic necessities.

It's a relatively modest contribution towards a much bigger challenge. But it reflects a belief that has guided Proud Partners for more than two decades.

Sometimes, creating opportunity begins with removing an obstacle.

Because a student's future should never depend on something as basic as being able to afford sanitary products.








Temperance Town Primary School Page:

Temperance Town Primary School


Faced with overcrowded classrooms and a shortage of teaching assistants, the staff at Temperance Town Primary School near Gordon's Bay came up with an extraordinary solution. They would pay for additional assistants out of their own salaries.

Unfortunately, they could only afford six of the seven assistants they needed.

Enter Clarence October, a 22-year-old Unisa teaching student who volunteered to fill the gap. For three years, she worked with learners in Grades 4 to 7 without receiving a cent.

When Proud Partners heard her story, we decided that such dedication deserved a little help. From March 2014, we funded Clarence's salary until she completed her teaching qualification and left the school in 2016.

That same spirit of commitment extended throughout Temperance Town. Under principal Gwen Opperman, the school received three Department of Education awards for academic progress, while a feeding programme led by Dr Elsa Verwey and the Dutch Reformed Church in Gordon's Bay, together with the departmental scheme, provided more than 500 children with nutritious daily meals.

For Proud Partners, supporting Clarence was a relatively small contribution to a school where people were already doing extraordinary things.

And sometimes, that's exactly what partnership is.















Stellenzicht Secondary School Reading Project Page:

Stellenzicht Secondary School Reading Project

Every word is a step forward.

It takes a village to raise a child. And in Jamestown, just outside Stellenbosch, an entire community took that saying to heart when it came to helping learners improve their reading, spelling and comprehension skills.

In 2015, the Friends of Stellenzicht, led by Dale Simons, joined forces with principal Lester Allies and his staff to introduce additional literacy lessons for Grades 8 and 9.

Retired teacher Alfred Christians joined the initiative in 2016, when more than 60 learners were identified as needing extra help. He was soon joined by local resident Ria Engelbrecht, who volunteered her time to provide individual attention to those who needed it most.

What began with some reluctance from learners soon developed into genuine enthusiasm. Reading aloud, spelling exercises and individual lessons helped build skills and confidence. And then came the competitions.

With financial support from Proud Partners, the school introduced incentives and prizes for StellenSpel, its spelling competition, StellenStorie for short stories and StellenDig for poetry. There was even StellenBundel, a collection of learners' writing illustrated by two talented young artists from the school.

What started as additional reading lessons became something much more: an opportunity for learners to discover the pleasure of words and take pride in their achievements.

And for Proud Partners, another opportunity to support dedicated people like Alfred and Ria, who were already making remarkable things happen.

Mathematics Support Project

When the numbers began to add up.

Eight volunteers. One determined organiser. And a mathematics pass rate of 94.4%.

In 2019, Nolubabalo Noji was determined to give learners the extra help they needed to succeed in mathematics. Her solution was an intensive study camp during the June school holidays, where eight volunteer mathematics teachers and Stellenbosch University students provided individual tuition, helped learners tackle difficult problems and worked through past examination papers.

Proud Partners contributed towards the cost of accommodation and study facilities, but there were still meals, transport and other expenses to consider.

Not one to be discouraged, Nolubabalo organised a fundraising campaign, including a Funky Socks and Hats concert performed by the learners themselves.

The result? A remarkable 94.4% pass rate, which even encouraged more learners to choose mathematics as a subject.

Proud Partners subsequently treated the eight volunteer tutors to a celebratory lunch, presenting each with a Certificate of Appreciation.

Although COVID-19 prevented the camp from taking place in 2020, the volunteers continued helping learners through weekend classes.

It's remarkable what one determined woman, eight generous volunteers and a little community spirit can achieve.

Nolubabalo, we salute you.










Girl Talk Initiative Project Page:

Girl Talk Initiative

Growing up shouldn't mean missing out.

For many young girls, starting their periods brings enough questions and uncertainties without having to worry whether they can afford basic sanitary products. Yet for some learners in the Stellenbosch area, that was exactly the problem. Some were even missing school because they had no suitable products to manage their periods.

Enter Girl Talk.

An initiative of BecomingKids, an early intervention programme managed by Stellumthombo, an initiative of the Moederkerk, Stellenbosch, Girl Talk set out to help girls navigate this important stage of their lives with greater confidence and dignity.

With sponsorship from Proud Partners, the first event was held at Cloetesville Primary School for 44 girls aged between 11 and 14. Arina Scheepers, the driving force behind BecomingKids, led an educational and motivational session, while volunteer Alta van Zyl added music, dancing and plenty of fun.

Each girl received a hamper worth R220, containing specially designed menstrual underwear and nine washable, reusable pads.

The initiative proved so popular that it was repeated at A.F. Louw Primary School, where another 43 girls participated. This time, Oh So Heavenly joined in, generously donating body creams and sprays.

Two schools. 87 girls. And a little practical assistance that meant far more than the contents of a hamper.

Because growing up should be something to celebrate, not a reason to miss school.

Domestic Partnership Project

A partnership begins at home.

The idea behind Proud Partners didn't begin in a boardroom or at a fundraising event. It began in a home, with two women who had shared twenty years of their lives.

When Selina Radebe presented Isabel Verwey with a certificate celebrating their long-standing partnership, she gave a name to something that Isabel had come to value enormously: the importance of recognising one another's needs, sharing experiences and taking an interest in each other's lives.

That simple idea would eventually inspire Proud Partners. And one of its earliest initiatives, the Domestic Partnership Project.

A working relationship.

When Proud Partners was established in 2004, South Africa was still finding its way through the enormous social and economic inequalities inherited from the past.

Isabel believed that one way to encourage greater understanding was to start with relationships that already existed. In particular, those between domestic workers and their employers.

These relationships often brought people from very different backgrounds into regular contact, yet that familiarity didn't necessarily mean they understood one another's circumstances, challenges or aspirations.

Proud Partners wanted to encourage people to look beyond their daily routines, listen to one another and find practical ways to make a positive contribution.

Not through grand gestures or financial handouts, but through mutual respect, consideration and the willingness to share time, knowledge and experience.

Sometimes, the smallest things matter.

The Domestic Partnership Project encouraged better communication and a more personal understanding of the people involved.

Proud Partners developed questionnaires intended to help employers and domestic workers discuss their expectations, working arrangements and everyday concerns.

It also encouraged practical assistance where welcomed, from adult education and financial literacy to information on healthcare, retirement planning and opportunities for further study.

An important part of the initiative focused on children and education.

Isabel had discovered through her experiences with Selina's family how easily young people's educational opportunities could be affected by subject choices, limited information and difficulties accessing bursaries.

It prompted Proud Partners to commission researcher Estelle du Toit to compile practical articles on tertiary education and financial assistance, helping families navigate what could be a confusing system.

Practical ideas for everyday life.

The project also explored ways of making everyday household budgets stretch further.

Working with North-West University, Proud Partners developed a comprehensive Afrikaans booklet of meal plans, recipes and nutritional guidelines designed for families with limited resources.

It included practical advice on meal planning, affordable ingredients, food preparation and ways of reducing household expenses.

Together with the educational articles and questionnaires, these resources reflected the organisation's original approach: identifying everyday challenges and finding practical ways to address them.

The documents now form part of Proud Partners' historical archive, reflecting both the concerns and the solutions of their time.

A philosophy that still matters.

Much has changed since the Domestic Partnership Project began, including greater recognition of domestic workers' employment rights in South Africa and the importance of fair working conditions.

Its central message remains relevant.

A respectful relationship begins with recognising one another as individuals, listening without assumptions and understanding that everyone has their own hopes, responsibilities and challenges.

Fair employment, personal dignity and clear boundaries must always come first. From that foundation, trust and mutual understanding can grow.

For Proud Partners, the Domestic Partnership Project was an early expression of an idea that would influence its many other initiatives.

Real partnerships are built on the things we do for one another.

David Cyster Page:

Copy:


David Cyster
Music Projects | Kuils River, Western Cape


The right instrument. At the right time.

Some children dream of becoming firefighters. Others want to be astronauts. At the age of three, David Cyster knew exactly what he wanted to do.

He wanted to play the clarinet.

He had heard the instrument on television, fallen in love with its sound and never looked back.

At seven, he began studying music at the Hugo Lambrechts Music Centre in Parow. By seventeen, the quiet young musician from Kuils River was already making quite a noise on South Africa's classical music scene.

In 2017, David won the Leon Hartshorne Prize for Best Woodwind Instrumentalist at the National Youth Music Competition, followed by several other awards and distinctions. He attended prestigious masterclasses, performed at concerts and festivals and earned a place in the Cape Town Philharmonic Youth Orchestra.

There was, however, one problem.

An instrument out of reach.

Although David was an accomplished clarinettist, he needed an A clarinet to perform much of the orchestral repertoire he was now encountering. Without one, he had to transpose music for the instrument he owned, adding unnecessary technical challenges to already demanding pieces.

For a young musician with David's ambitions, the right instrument could make an enormous difference.

His mother, Helen, approached Proud Partners.

After meeting David and discovering more about his remarkable talent and dedication, Proud Partners purchased a nearly new A clarinet for him.

According to Helen, her normally shy son was almost beside himself with excitement. She later described the gift as something that had quite literally changed his life.

And David showed his appreciation in the best way he knew how.

He kept practising.

And the music played on.
